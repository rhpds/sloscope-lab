== Learning objectives

* Form a bounded reliability assessment from a measured change and its comparison reference.
* Choose collection or repair when evidence cannot support a verdict.

== Show: A larger error count creates a decision, not an explanation

The Example Retail product owner returns with an edited scenario: 2300 errors instead of 230, against the same 100000 synthetic requests. "That looks worse. Does it justify lowering the target so the promotion can proceed? Is the new release the cause?"

The count warrants investigation, but neither proposed conclusion follows from it. Lowering a target does not repair checkout behavior. Naming the release as the cause needs evidence that this artifact does not contain. Your job is to give the team a reliability assessment that states the measured concern, its limits, and the next action.

No real service was changed. The scenario deliberately edits public fixture data; it is not evidence of a real incident or a new production collection window. The original `lookback_window` and query start/end timestamps are retained. The changed `collected_at` marks the scenario artifact, not a fresh telemetry query. Treat this as a counterfactual comparison of the same fixture, not independent before/after samples. Record that limitation under comparability in your decision brief.

== Learn: Drift, impact, and cause are different conclusions

Drift compares an observed indicator with a reference. Here, deterministic code computes deviations and checks tolerance bands around that reference. A band breach is a signal to review. It is not automatically a breach of a customer SLO, and the tolerance-band policy is not the customer's contract.

=== Worked example: Describe the change without exaggerating it

The error ratio changes from `230 / 100000 = 0.0023` to `2300 / 100000 = 0.023`. In percentage terms, that is *0.23% to 2.3%*: a 10-fold ratio and an increase of *2.07 percentage points*. Those descriptions are compatible; "2.07% more errors" would be ambiguous.

The engine also derives availability as `1 - error ratio`. It therefore changes from 99.77% to 97.7%. Error and availability breaches share the same error count. They are not independent proof of 2 faults, and they do not show how many customers abandoned checkout or why the errors occurred.

SLOscope's evidence policy then asks whether the signal is admissible for interpretation: finite measurements, known identities, sufficient reported coverage, and agreement between measured values and breach labels. `admissible` means those implemented checks passed. It does not mean "release approved" or "cause proven." The input digest ties the check to this exact artifact.

=== Worked example: Abstention is a review outcome

The current admission policy requires at least 0.8 reported measured coverage. At 0.79, the correct next step is more evidence or a coverage investigation. Rewording the conclusion or selecting a more confident model cannot supply missing measurements.

A second failure is contradiction: the measurements say there are breaches, but the summary list says there are none. That input needs repair before interpretation. An abstention keeps either problem from becoming a confident recommendation.

* "The measured error ratio increased; investigate before approving a target change."
* "The release caused checkout abandonment; lower the SLO to proceed."

Here is 1 row to read in that JSON. For `error_rate_ratio`, `baseline_value` is 0.0023 and `live_value` is 0.023; `direction` is `increasing`, `band_breach` is `true`, and `first_pass_class` is `error_rate_elevation`. The `band_lower` and `band_upper` values define the comparison range around the reference. The changed value exceeds its upper bound. You can see a concern without asserting its cause.

The overall `dominant_signal.class` is `availability_drop` in this pinned scenario. The engine selects by normalized breach magnitude, not by business priority. Both breaches come from the same error count; the dominant label is not a second independent fault. This is a deterministic first-pass label, not a report generated by a model. The following `review` result is the policy admission result. Neither output is a causal diagnosis.

==== Verify the measurement and the assessment

Technical check: `drift` checked the reference hash before and after comparison. The original remains in place, and both breached indicators appear in the signal. A modified reference would fail the check.

Decision check: the first statement is supported within this synthetic scenario. Record the changed ratios and shared-input limitation. Do not name a cause or approve the promotion from these measurements alone. A real decision would also need the applicable SLO, affected-user context, release/change evidence, and the team's operational review.

=== Exercise 2: Choose a next step when evidence is inadequate

*Predict:* For coverage 0.79, choose one next action: collect or repair evidence; change the target; or continue because the model can fill the gap. Explain why your choice protects the review.

Create the low-coverage challenge and require its exact rejection:

[source,role="execute"]
----
.lab/venv/bin/python scripts/labctl.py low-coverage
status=0
result=$(.lab/venv/bin/python scripts/labctl.py review --input .lab/learner/low-coverage-signal.json) || status=$?
[ "$status" -eq 2 ] && [[ "$result" == *"At least 0.8 measured coverage"* ]] || {
  printf '%s\n' "$result"
  echo 'FAIL: did not observe the intended coverage abstention'
  exit 1
}
echo 'PASS: insufficient coverage abstained'
----

Expected result: the review abstains with a reason requiring more evidence, and the shell prints `PASS: insufficient coverage abstained`. The review's nonzero exit is intentional. The shell verifies its specific reason; it does not treat every failure as a successful check.

Now consider contradictory summaries. *Predict:* If the measured breaches remain but their summary list is empty, is this "no drift" or an input that needs repair?

[source,role="execute"]
----
.lab/venv/bin/python scripts/labctl.py conflict
status=0
result=$(.lab/venv/bin/python scripts/labctl.py review --input .lab/learner/conflicting-signal.json) || status=$?
[ "$status" -eq 2 ] && [[ "$result" == *"Breached indicator list conflicts"* ]] || {
  printf '%s\n' "$result"
  echo 'FAIL: did not observe the intended consistency abstention'
  exit 1
}
echo 'PASS: conflicting breach list abstained'
----

Expected result: abstention explaining that the breach list conflicts with measured evidence, followed by `PASS: conflicting breach list abstained`.

==== Verify the policy and the next action

Technical check: both challenges abstain for the intended reasons. An import error or missing file is an environment failure and must not be reported as evidence-policy success.

Decision check: both cases require collection or repair. Neither says the service is healthy. No model interpretation can turn these failed prerequisites into an approved target change. In a real review, assign ownership for repairing the coverage or contradictory artifact before returning to the decision.

== Takeaway: State what you know and what you need

In the *Reliability assessment* section of your decision brief, record:

* The measured error change in counts, ratios, and percentage points, tied to the preserved reference and input digest.
* Why the availability signal is related evidence rather than a second independent fault.
* One supported concern, one causal or customer-impact claim that remains unproven, and the evidence needed to examine it.
* Your next action for low coverage and contradictory summaries, including who must supply corrected evidence in a real review.

The workplace value is a review another engineer can challenge without guessing what the numbers mean. In module 3, you will evaluate whether a technically renderable alert threshold is also a defensible recommendation.

== Troubleshooting

If a challenge unexpectedly succeeds, retain the JSON artifacts and source commit and report the regression. Do not rewrite the input to obtain a preferred classification. If ordinary review fails, inspect identity, coverage, finite numbers, and consistency before configuring any model.

== Learning outcomes

You should now be able to separate measured deterioration from customer impact and cause, interpret an admissibility stamp without treating it as approval, and choose a useful next action when the evidence requires abstention.
