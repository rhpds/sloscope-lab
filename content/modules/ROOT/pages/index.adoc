= Review service reliability evidence with SLOscope
include::partial$_attributes.adoc[]

A checkout service can look healthy in an average while its error rate is changing. Before changing a service level objective (SLO), your site reliability engineering (SRE) team needs to know which measurements support the decision, which claims remain uncertain, and when to stop for more evidence.

This hands-on lab teaches that review process with SLOscope. You inspect telemetry artifacts, preserve a reference, measure drift, challenge inconsistent inputs, and test an export boundary. You can also review the same artifacts through the optional Model Context Protocol (MCP) adapter.

IMPORTANT: The core exercises use public, synthetic data and recorded demonstrations. They do not query a production service, make real model calls, prove alert behavior, or approve service commitments. The live qualification checklist remains a separate delivery gate.

== Learning objectives

* Trace a decision to its service, telemetry window, coverage, and input digest.
* Compare a changed measurement with a preserved reference.
* Demonstrate abstention for insufficient or conflicting evidence.
* Explain why an export preview and an advisory model response still need human review.

== Your route through the lab

Allow about 100 minutes for the core path and 20 minutes for the optional MCP extension. These are pacing estimates; you can work at your own speed.

== Learning outcomes

After the lab, you should be able to explain why measured evidence, interpretation, and approval are different steps, and identify the evidence that is still missing before a real deployment.
